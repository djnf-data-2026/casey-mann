Arkansas prisons and their host counties

This story will examine how reported census demographics differ from everyday life in prison counties by visualizing the racial makeup when prison populations are counted versus when they are not. It will also explore the financial impact of hosting a prison by comparing prison county median incomes to the median incomes of surrounding counties.

Why now?

To much debate from residents and lawmakers alike, Arkansas Governor Sarah Huckabee Sanders announced plans for the construction of a 3,000 bed prison in rural Franklin County in October 2024. Two years later, construction is stalled, but it is unclear what effect the facility could have on it’s host county.

Target Audience

This project aims to serve both rural Arkansans and policy makers by outlining the impact that hosting a prison can have on a community and therefore assisting in decision making.

Potential Interviews

  1. Business owners in Franklin County. Do they feel like the facility could push industry or customers away?
  2. Franklin County residents. How do they feel about the potential build? Does the presence of the proposed facility change how they feel about their home? What about their plans for the future? Will anyone want to move if construction is resumed or completed?
  3. Residents of Malvern, Arkansas, home to the most recent prison build in the state. Despite occurring more than 20 years ago, some residents may still recall how the facility changed the area. Potential for solid anecdotal details.
  4. Lawmakers on both sides of the argument for the facility’s construction.
    • FOR: Republican Sen. Jonathan Dismang of Searcy, who filed an appropriations bill during the 2025 session that would have provided $750 million in spending authority for the prison project. google
    • AGAINST: Republican Sen. Bryan King of Green Forest, who has been an outspoken critic of plans to construct the Franklin County facility. google

Potential Impact

This story has the potential to impact both Franklin County residents and Arkansas policy. Residents who may feel like they are in the dark about what is happening in their own community could get access to data-backed context that informs decisions about whether they support the build. The story also serves to provide insight into how Franklin could be affected long-term if policymakers follow through on the build.

Arkansas policymakers could get an empirical look at how the Franklin County facility will change the lives of those who live in the area, potentially affecting how they vote on the issue in a moment when the future of the project appears to be up in the air.

I then worked to identify which counties are home to one or more correctional facilities and add this information to my table of ranked county incomes.

The facilities csv is listed in census tracts, but we can get county info from that. A census tract include county indentifiers in the number. The first 5 characters identify the state and county. For example a code like 05069002103 breaks down as 05 (Arkansas), 069 (Jefferson County), and 002103.

prisons_counties <- prisons %>%
  mutate(GEOID = str_sub(census_tract, 1, 5)) %>%  
  distinct(GEOID) %>%
  mutate(has_prison = "Yes")
ar_income_ranked <- ar_income_ranked %>%
  left_join(prisons_counties, by = "GEOID") %>%
  mutate(has_prison = replace_na(has_prison, "No"))
prison_counts <- prisons %>%
  mutate(GEOID = str_sub(census_tract, 1, 5)) %>%
  group_by(GEOID) %>%
  summarise(num_prisons = n())

ar_income_ranked %>%
  inner_join(prison_counts, by = "GEOID") %>%
  arrange(rank) %>%
  select(rank, county, median_income, num_prisons)

Previous coverage

Coverage of the Franklin County prison has been largely dominated by details of the cost of the construction itself, but I cannot seem to find much detail on any potential long-term impacts of the build. Much of the coverage contains dissenting opinions from Franklin County residents, who often express concern that the facility could lower quality of life and property values. The potential build gained national attention when Franklin County Sheriff Johnny Crocker began to criticize the plans, saying several Arkansas counties requested the build, and Franklin was not one of them. He also said the United States Immigration and Customers Enforcement was considering using the proposed prison site as a detention facility.
